📚 Research Worth Reading

JevAgentHarness: decision models that share control with LLM agents

Problem: Agent harnesses call a large LLM on almost every step even when the action is a multiple-choice tool decision.

Key idea: Delegate high-confidence tool choices to a small decision model with trajectory-fitted thresholds, and train AgentDecision-4B on states after agent mistakes.

Why developers should care: On out-of-domain tasks the harness kept 91.5% success while cutting cost up to 89% and latency up to 2.5× in reported setups.

Paper: arXiv:2610.21412

SpecAgent: trajectory-level speculation with semantic verification

Problem: Strong models decode every tool step even when a weak model could draft routine read-only segments.

Key idea: A weak model drafts grounded tool trajectories; the strong model verifies prefixes in one prefill pass and repairs the first rejected step.

Why developers should care: On τ²-Bench the method nearly matched strong-model success while roughly halving wall time and memory traffic in the paper.

Paper: arXiv:2610.21463

CoPE: co-evolving acting and forecasting for language agents

Problem: Agent RL often wastes expensive rollouts because only a terminal scalar reward supervises intermediate steps.

Key idea: Train the policy to forecast upcoming action sequences on successful trajectories, creating multi-step action targets alongside standard RL.

Why developers should care: The method improved aggregate scores across ALFWorld, WebShop, τ²-bench, and AppWorld versus GRPO in the reported experiments without extra inference overhead.

Paper: arXiv:2610.20597
